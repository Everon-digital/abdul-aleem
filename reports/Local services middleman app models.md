# Winning middlemen charge for outcomes, not leads

A new local-services middleman app has real room to work in, but not as another directory or shared-lead seller: the opening is to own the steps between "I found a provider" and "the job is done and paid for", and to charge the provider only on a verified outcome. Every incumbent model leaves that space open. Pay-per-call networks sell a phone call for a spread and bill on crude duration rules; Google lists businesses for free, hands the customer off to a phone number and since 2025 has dropped its money-back guarantee while billing providers for missed calls; lead marketplaces such as Angi and Thumbtack charge providers before any sign the customer will hire, which is the single most consistent provider complaint in every country researched. Commission marketplaces align incentives better but leak: customers and providers cut the platform out after the first job, which helped kill Homejoy in the US and Sukoon in Pakistan. The markets are large and mostly offline (India is about US$60B with under 1% booked online), and outside the US the real competitor is the WhatsApp group and the neighbour's recommendation, not another app. The strongest evidence-backed design is consumer-chosen, exclusive matching; payment per qualified contact or booked job with automatic credits; AI qualification in front of the hand-off; and a trust layer (verified identity, transaction-tied reviews, escrow or redo guarantee) that gives both sides a reason to stay on-platform. Two cautions apply throughout: most pricing data in this space comes from vendors with something to sell, and there is almost no reliable data on Pakistan or the Gulf, so willingness to pay there is unproven.

## Pay-per-call networks earn the spread on a phone call

Pay-per-call is the purest form of the middleman business, and its mechanics are worth understanding because they are the template a call- or chat-based app would borrow. A publisher (a website, an ad campaign or a map listing) generates an inbound call on a tracking number. A routing platform such as Ringba then either sends the call by fixed rules or auctions it: the call is "pinged" to every buyer at once, bids come back within **2 to 5 seconds**, and the highest bidder usually wins ([Ringba](https://www.ringba.com/blog/pay-per-call/real-time-bidding-for-calls)). The contractor pays only if the call meets a contractual "billable" definition. The network's profit is simply what the buyer pays per qualified call minus what the publisher is paid ([CallScaler](https://callscaler.com/blog/call-tracking-for-pay-per-call)). Software platforms charge for usage and carry no quality risk; networks carry the dispute and credit risk. A middleman app is structurally a network, not a software platform.

The billable definition is the most contested term in the industry, and it varies by a factor of ten. Typical thresholds are 60 to 120 seconds of talk time, with a 10 to 30 second grace window ([Elevarus](https://elevarus.com/pay-per-call-buffer-time-settings-by-vertical/)). eLocal's rule is far looser: a call is billable if it **rang for 10 seconds or more**, even if the caller hangs up or the buyer declines it, and credits go to account balance rather than back to the card ([eLocal](https://help.elocal.com/features/valid-invalid-calls-leads/)). These rules are where contractor resentment comes from, and they are a positioning opportunity for a fairer intermediary.

US prices show how much a single qualified contact is worth, and how wide the gap is between what the contractor pays and what the call generator receives. The figures below come from different companies' data sets, so the implied spread is illustrative, not a measured take rate; no network publishes its margin.

|---|---|---|---|
| Plumbing | $60-$255 | $33.25 | $66.17 |
| HVAC / air conditioning | $65-$325 | $38.50 | $50.21 |
| Roofing | $85-$550 | $42.00 | $101.15 |
| Appliance repair | $22-$85 | $14.00 | not reported |
| Locksmith | $15-$75 | not reported | not reported |

Sources: [Service Direct](https://servicedirect.com/pay-per-lead-costs/), [Lead Smart payouts](https://leadsmartinc.com/news/press-google-lsa-lead-costs-rise-payout-data-september-2026), [Lead Smart LSA data](https://pr.davisjournal.com/article/Lead-Smart-Inc-Google-Lead-Costs-Rose-Up-to-69percent-in-a-Year-What-the-Same-Calls-Pay-Publishers/6a9d61185e47130002855182). The Service Direct ranges were read from a partly jumbled search summary and should be re-checked on the page.

Service Direct says **50% to 60% of billable leads become booked appointments** ([Service Direct](https://support.servicedirect.com/hubfs/Product%20Marketing/Info%20Sheets%20and%20User%20Guides/Service%20Direct%20Marketplace%20for%20Clients/ServiceDirect-Marketplace-Clients-Success-HomeServices.pdf)). At that rate a $100 plumbing call costs the contractor $170 to $200 per booking, which only works on jobs worth several hundred dollars. That is why low-ticket urgent trades sit at the bottom of the price range and roofing, HVAC replacement and water damage at the top, and why Lead Smart's August 2026 benchmark found that **every top-paying category now prices per appointment, not per call duration** ([Lead Smart](https://pr.portlandtribune.com/article/Lead-Smart-Inc-Publishes-August-2026-Pay-Per-Call-Payout-Benchmark-for-417983-ZIP-Service-Rows/6a7de4e775104c0002c9a2c6)). The industry is moving from "a call happened" to "an appointment was booked".

The model has two structural weaknesses a consumer app avoids. The first is supply. Much call inventory comes from "rank and rent" operators who build generic websites and Google Business Profiles, attach a tracking number and sell the calls. Google treats this as a policy violation: one lawsuit alleged a single operator was tied to more than 350 fake profiles ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-lawsuit-rank-and-rent/489666/)), and an account suspension can wipe out an operator's whole inventory overnight ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/google-business-profile-suspensions/)). The second is fraud: robocalls, duplicate callers and short calls engineered to pass the duration threshold are endemic ([Anura](https://www.anura.io/blog/topic/call-fraud)). An app with its own brand and returning users is a first-party source of demand and sidesteps both problems.

AI is now moving the value from owning the phone number to owning the qualification step. AI voice agents answer, check service type, location and urgency, and then transfer or book, at a quoted cost of **$0.07 to $0.31 per minute** ([Retell AI](https://www.retellai.com/use-cases/ai-lead-qualification)), which is trivial against a $30 to $250 call. The uplift claims are all vendor marketing; no independent data exists on conversion gains or on how many callers hang up when greeted by a machine. On regulation, inbound calls (the consumer dials) carry little exposure under the US Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA), while any outbound AI-voiced call needs prior consent, because the FCC ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voices count as "artificial" under that law ([FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-makes-ai-generated-voices-robocalls-illegal)). The FCC's stricter "one-to-one consent" rule for lead sellers was struck down by a federal appeals court in January 2025 and never took effect ([Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule)).

## Google lists everyone for free, then steps out of the transaction

Google Maps is the de facto directory, with more than 2 billion monthly users ([On The Map](https://www.onthemap.com/?p=56947)), and its model has two layers. The free layer is the Google Business Profile: a listing ranked on relevance, distance and prominence, where practitioners say the primary category, the searcher's proximity and keywords in the business name matter most, with reviews next ([Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)). The paid layer is Local Services Ads (LSA), a pay-per-lead unit above the map results where the provider pays for each call or message rather than each click ([Invoca](https://www.invoca.com/blog/googles-local-services-ads-move-to-performance-max-what-home-services-businesses-need-to-know)).

The important point for a new entrant is what Google does not do. The user sees a rating, reviews, photos and hours, then taps "call" and leaves. Google has no view of the quote, the job, the price or the outcome. It has also been removing what little interaction it hosted: chat and call history ended on **31 July 2024** ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14919056?hl=en)), and the customer Q&A section was retired in late 2025 in favour of an AI "Ask" button ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-maps-qa-feature-ask-40594.html)). Where messaging survives, Google now points customers to the business's own SMS or WhatsApp number, which amounts to an admission that the conversation lives elsewhere.

On the paid side, each change of the past two years has moved lead-quality risk from Google to the provider. Trade sources report that the Google Guaranteed badge was replaced in October 2025 by a single "Google Verified" badge, and that the money-back guarantee went with it ([CI Web Group](https://www.ciwebgroup.com/blog/google-replaces-google-guaranteed-with-the-new-google-verified-badge--what-contractors-need-to-know)). Manual lead disputes gave way to automated credits, and the "wrong job type" and "wrong area" credit categories were removed ([Footbridge Media](https://www.footbridgemedia.com/marketing-tips/5-changes-google-local-service-ads-contractors-should-know)). From **1 October 2026**, a missed call during business hours is billable if the caller waits more than 20 seconds ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-charges-local-services-advertisers-for-missed-calls-over-20-seconds/)). And from August 2026 Google began folding LSA into its automated Performance Max campaigns, ending manual per-lead bids and dropping historical reports ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-folds-local-services-ads-into-google-ads-cuts-historical-reports/)).

The price benchmark this sets is concrete. A February 2026 study of 888 contractors put the blended home-services LSA cost at about **$53 per lead**; for HVAC, roughly $51 per lead at a 44% booking rate works out to about **$116 per booked job** ([Enrich Labs](https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/google-local-service-ads-complete-guide-2026); [Elevarus](https://elevarus.com/hvac-lead-generation-cost-per-lead-2026-benchmarks/)). That is what a US provider already pays before doing any work, with no guarantee of being hired, and it is the number a new app's fee competes against in the US. These economics do not transfer to markets where a plumbing visit costs a few dollars; whether LSA is even available in India, the UAE or Saudi Arabia was not verified.

Trust in the directory itself is under strain. Google blocked or removed more than **292 million fake reviews and 13 million fake business profiles in 2025** ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-maps-spam-fighting-2025-41176.html)), and urgent trades such as locksmiths are the worst affected ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/google-maps-fake-listings-lawsuit-scams/)). Regulators are active: the US Federal Trade Commission's review rule carries penalties of up to $53,088 per violation ([Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-enforcement-under-consumer-review-rule/)), and the UK competition authority extracted formal undertakings from Google on fake reviews in January 2025 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-secures-important-changes-from-google-to-tackle-fake-reviews)). Reviews also work against newcomers: **47% of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and 74% only care about reviews from the last three months** ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)). A new app faces that same cold start and needs a different trust signal than star counts.

Two further developments shape the opening. First, discovery is shifting to AI. Google launched "Ask Maps" in March 2026, a conversational layer that answers from profile data and reviews ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-business-profile-qa)), and secondary coverage of BrightLocal's 2026 survey says the share of consumers using AI tools for local recommendations rose from 6% to 45% in a year while Google's share fell from 83% to 71%. That figure was not confirmed on BrightLocal's own pages and should be treated as indicative ([Jasmine Directory](https://www.jasminedirectory.com/blog/local-consumer-review-survey-2026/)). Second, Google has already built the obvious AI middleman feature: since 16 July 2025 it will phone local businesses on a user's behalf to check prices and availability, free, across 45 US states ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/16/google-rolls-out-ai-powered-business-calling-feature-brings-gemini-2-5-pro-to-ai-mode); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2025/google-adds-ai-powered-local-business-calling-search)). A startup cannot win in the US on "AI collects quotes" alone. The feature was reported as US and English only, so the same idea in Urdu, Hindi or Arabic over WhatsApp is not something Google offers today.

Finally, Google's data cannot be the app's foundation. The Places API is billed per request (third-party guides cite $32 per 1,000 text searches at the Pro tier), and Google's terms allow only the place ID to be stored indefinitely, with coordinates cached for at most 30 days ([Google Maps Platform](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/geocoding/policies); [Open Places API](https://openplacesapi.com/blog/google-places-api-pricing)). Google listings can seed onboarding and be shown live with attribution, but the durable asset has to be first-party: verified identities, real prices, job history and the app's own reviews.

## Three marketplace models, each with a predictable failure

Existing marketplaces fall into three families, and the pattern across them is consistent: the closer the fee sits to the start of the funnel, the higher the margin and the angrier the providers; the closer it sits to the completed job, the better the alignment and the worse the leakage.

| Model | Examples | How it earns | Scale and health | Where it fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lead-gen and advertising | Angi, Thumbtack, Yelp, Justdial, Bark, Checkatrade, Google LSA | Provider pays per lead, per click or per subscription, before any job is won | Angi about $1.03B in 2025, down 13%; Thumbtack nearly $500M, up 24%; Justdial about Rs 1,214 crore, up 6% | Provider pays for leads that never hire; shared leads; provider churn; regulatory action |
| Commission on completed job | Airtasker, TaskRabbit | Percentage of each paid job | Airtasker takes about 22% of A$240M in bookings | Repeat customers go direct to avoid the fee |
| Managed, full-stack | Urban Company | Platform sets price, trains and assigns workers, takes commission plus product sales | Revenue Rs 1,555 crore, up 36%; keeps about 36% of transaction value | Worker protests, capital intensity, a decade to reach profit |

Sources: [Panabee on Angi](https://www.panabee.com/news/angi-earnings-2025-annual), [Fast Company on Thumbtack](https://www.fastcompany.com/91534514/), [Outlook Business on Justdial](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/just-dial-q4-results-operating-revenue-hits-307-cr-profit-dips-on-treasury-drag), [Airtasker ASX filing](https://bulletin.webull.com/qbd/announcement/20260826/499500381/09acc31376e150e92d7a425d9f6e2ba9.pdf), [Whalesbook on Urban Company](https://www.whalesbook.com/corporate-news/English/tech/Urban-Company-FY26-Revenue-Surges-36percent-to-indian-rupee1556-Cr-Loss-Narrows/69fdbb0b81f0ecc2dc72f3e7). Thumbtack is private; its figures are management statements, not audited.

### Lead-gen is the most profitable model and the most resented

Lead-gen produces the biggest revenue pools and the best margins: Thumbtack reports gross margins above 90% and EBITDA profitability since 2023 ([Built In](https://builtin.com/company/thumbtack/faq/stability-growth)). The cost falls on providers. Pros pay per lead whether or not the job closes and compete with several others for the same customer ([Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/?p=83716)); vendor guides say a standard Angi lead is shared among three to eight contractors ([LeadTruffle](https://wwwleadtruffleco.pages.dev/blog/complete-guide-angi-leads-home-service-contractors-2026)). Customers do not know the pro paid to speak to them, so they ghost freely ([Thumbtack Community](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/comment/5845)). Complaints are uniform across markets: dead leads, shared leads, opaque and rising prices, out-of-area matches and refunds that arrive as credits. Much of this evidence comes from competing vendors and review sites, so the intensity is likely overstated, but the pattern is corroborated by regulators. The FTC found HomeAdvisor misrepresented the quality and source of its leads and ordered up to **$7.2 million** in redress in 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/23/the-ftc-fines-homeadvisor-up-to-7-2m-for-lying-about-lead-quality-and-other-matters)).

Angi then ran the experiment that prices the shared-lead model. In January 2025 it switched to "homeowner choice", where the customer selects which pros receive the request. Leads from its partner network fell **78%** and network revenue fell **79%** year on year by Q4 2025, while leads from its own channels rose 25% ([Angi IR](https://ir.angi.com/node/13006/html)). Total revenue dropped 13% for the year, active pros fell 19% to about 122,000, and management later withdrew guidance ([Panabee](https://www.panabee.com/news/angi-earnings-2025-annual); [Quartr](https://quartr.com/events/angi-inc-angi-q1-2026_3e5aFGrf)). Yet by Q3 2026 revenue of $242.0M was down 8.9% while adjusted EBITDA rose 6.3% ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-angi-q3-earnings-call-highlights-2026-11-04/)). The reading is that a large share of the old revenue came from selling one customer to pros the customer never chose, and that the leads which remain are worth more. A new app should start where Angi was forced to end.

### Commission and managed models align incentives but leak

Marketplaces that process the payment cluster at a 20% to 25% take: Airtasker about 22%, TaskRabbit a 15% service fee plus a roughly 7.5% trust fee charged to the client ([TaskRabbit](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-us/articles/204411610-What-are-the-Tasker-fees)). Urban Company, which sets prices and trains its workers, keeps about 36% of transaction value once product sales are included, and **about 82% of its transaction value comes from repeat customers** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/start-ups/urban-company-fy25-profit-revenue-growth-ipo-plans-125061801031_1.html)). That is the upside of control.

The downside is leakage, the tendency of customer and provider to transact directly once they have met. Homejoy, a US cleaning marketplace that raised about $40M, shut in 2015; its founder blamed worker-classification lawsuits, while a former employee pointed to costly expansion and the best cleaners leaving for direct arrangements ([Time](https://time.com/3963565/homejoy-cleaning-shuts-down/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homejoy)). Dawn described the same dynamic at Pakistan's Sukoon, where after a few jobs customer and worker cut the platform out to save the commission; Sukoon later closed ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1309231/can-pakistani-home-repair-startup-sukooncompk-live-up-to-its-name); [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1530589/lights-out-at-sukoon)). Practitioner guidance is that policing does not fix this; only value delivered at the moment of the transaction does, such as payment protection, insurance, scheduling and reviews ([Sharetribe](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/how-to-discourage-people-from-going-around-your-payment-system/)). Leakage is worst where the same person returns to the same home (cleaning, maids, beauty, tutoring) and mildest for one-off or urgent jobs.

Control brings its own costs. Urban Company cut its top commission from 30% to 25% after partner protests in 2021 and has faced repeated unrest over penalties and account blocking ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/urban-company-slashes-commission-by-5-percentage-after-workers-protest/65384.html); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/urban-company-rocked-by-another-protest-by-gig-workers-for-blocking-their-ids)). Founded in 2014, it posted its first full-year profit only in FY25 ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/06/urban-company-first-full-year-profit-revenue-jumps-38-ipo)), then swung back to a Rs 234.81 crore loss in FY26 on its instant house-help service, which lost about **Rs 447 per order** in the latest reported quarter ([Arihant Capital](https://www.arihantcapital.com/company-information/companynewsdetails/25131010204); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/urban-companys-instahelp-burn-per-order-climbs-to-447-calls-it-deliberate)). Of the eight Indian home-service startups launched around 2014-15, nearly all the others were shut, acquired or shrunk ([A Junior VC](https://www.ajuniorvc.com/insant-helps-snabbit-pronto-case-study-urban-company-market-unicorn)). The managed model works, but it is a capital game.

Incumbents are also diversifying away from the pure marketplace. Yelp's fastest-growing line, up 82%, includes an AI lead-response product for service businesses and data licensing ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/yelp-ad-revenue-drops-6-as-clicks-fall-8-in-third-quarter/)); Yelp licensed its reviews to OpenAI in July 2026 ([Axios](https://axios.com/2026/07/23/yelp-reviews-chatgpt-geo-partnership)); Thumbtack and Angi both plugged into ChatGPT ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251006760229/en/Thumbtack-Partners-with-OpenAI-to-Power-Home-Services-in-ChatGPT); [Contractor Mag](https://www.contractormag.com/around-the-web/news/55361678/angi-expands-ai-strategy-with-chatgpt-app-integration)). The directory is becoming a data supplier to AI assistants.

## In India, Pakistan and the Gulf the rival is WhatsApp

Outside the US, true per-qualified-call pricing barely exists. Providers are sold visibility and shared leads on prepaid or subscription terms and bear the quality risk themselves. Justdial is the large-scale proof: 56.1 million listings but only **631,530 paid campaigns**, about 1.1% of listings, at roughly Rs 19,000 a year each ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/just-dial-reports-37-drop-in-q4-pat-126041400229_1.html); [Trendlyne](https://trendlyne.com/posts/5482420/q3fy26-quarterly-result-announced-for-just-dial-ltd)). Its traffic has been flat at about 193 million quarterly visitors while listings grew 13% ([Indian Television](https://indiantelevision.com/mam/justdial-begins-fy27-with-10-per-cent-revenue-growth-on-ai-led-momentum/)), which suggests suppliers want to be listed but consumer appetite for a pure directory is saturated. Sulekha sells prepaid lead credits, with each request sent to several providers; a reseller quotes Rs 150 to Rs 800 per lead, a figure not confirmed with Sulekha ([SmartAds](https://smartads.in/services/digital/sulekha-digital-advertising)). The UK looks similar: Bark sells each lead to up to five professionals ([Whito](https://whito.co.uk/trades/research/uk-trade-directory-costs/)). Lead prices one to two orders of magnitude below US levels mean the unit economics in South Asia must rest on volume, automation and cheap channels rather than telephony auctions.

The market itself is large and almost entirely offline. Redseer puts India's home-services market at about **US$60B with under 1% online**, and the top eight cities account for 85% to 90% of online demand ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/indias-online-home-services-market-to-grow-at-22-to-hit-88-billion-by-fy30-report); [HDFC Sky](https://hdfcsky.com/news/indias-online-home-services-market-to-be-indian-rupee-85-88-billion-by-fy30)). The fast-growing segment is instant house help, where three apps reached about 10 million monthly users combined ([Newsbytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/indias-instant-home-services-apps-reach-10-million-monthly-users/tldr)) while burning nearly **US$8M a month** between them ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/snabbit-says-the-discount-game-is-almost-over-the-hard-part-comes-next/)). That proves frequency can be created in home services, and that it is not a wedge for a lean entrant.

For Pakistan and the Gulf the evidence is thin and must be read as anecdote. No methodology-backed market size exists for either. In Pakistan, providers are commonly found through numbers forwarded in WhatsApp groups and recommendations from neighbours ([FatafatService](https://fatafatservice.com/blog/why-service-discovery-depends-on-contacts-fatafatservice)), and supply is described as mostly micro, offline players ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2410646)). Mahir Company self-reports more than 50,000 professionals and 300,000 app users and sells maintenance subscription packages ([Mahir Company](https://mahircompany.com/blog/?p=1231)); funding in the country is small and old, with Helpp's US$1.1M round the largest found. In the Gulf, Justlife claims more than 1 million users and 10 million services delivered across the UAE and Saudi Arabia ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/justlife-and-dac-partner-to-bring-cleaning-products-into-professional-home-services-across-the-uae-1460155)) and distributes through the Careem super-app ([tbreak](https://tbreak.com/careem-and-justlife-partner-to-launch-specialty-cleaning-health-moving-home-services-in-uae)), while Urban Company cites Saudi joint-venture losses as a drag on its results ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/start-up/news/urban-company-swings-to-losses-post-ipo-as-insta-help-investments-surge)). Regional press calls word of mouth the "safest bet" for homeowners ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/1554181)).

The implication is that in these markets Google Maps supplies a shortlist and a phone number, and the trust decision is made socially. An app has to import that social proof (referrals, neighbours' completed jobs) rather than rely on stars. It also has to assume that a per-job commission is the easiest fee to evade in a cash-heavy, WhatsApp-native market; the Sukoon story points that way. The absence of recent funded startups in Pakistan and the Gulf is either open ground or a warning, and the research cannot tell which.

## The open ground lies after discovery, and in how you charge

Three gaps recur across every model examined. Consumers rank trust above price: nearly 70% of US homeowners worry about unreliable contractors and 41% say a provider has deceived them ([Leaf Home survey](https://finanzen.net/nachricht/aktien/leaf-home-survey-reveals-trust-gap-in-home-improvement-industry-14185554)), and 97% expect a price before deciding ([HVACR Trends](https://hvacrtrends.com/housecall-pro-customer-service-report-home-contracting/?amp=1)) while only about a quarter of roofing contractors show one online ([Roofing Contractor](https://www.roofingcontractor.com/articles/100649-2025-homeowner-roofing-survey-tracking-the-journey)). Providers pay for risk they cannot control, and they also lose demand they already earned: vendor studies put unanswered calls to home-service businesses at **14% to 27%** ([Invoca](https://www.invoca.com/blog/how-much-missed-sales-calls-cost-home-services-businesses); [StealthAgents](https://stealthagents.com/research/home-services-missed-call-revenue-statistics-2026)); higher figures are AI-receptionist marketing. And no large platform owns the post-quote steps: Google no longer backs a guarantee, lead sellers stop at the introduction, and reviews are not tied to a paid job.

The monetization options trade margin against alignment and leakage. The mechanisms in the table are this report's synthesis of the cited cases, not sourced benchmarks.

| Option | Precedent | Strength | Weakness | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pay per qualified call or lead | Service Direct, Thumbtack | Paid before the job, so leakage is irrelevant; cash early | Provider bears conversion risk; resentment if shared or loosely defined | High-ticket, quote-first trades; US |
| Pay per booked appointment | Top-paying US pay-per-call categories | Closest to the outcome without touching payment | Platform carries no-show and attribution risk | Urgent, one-off repairs |
| Commission on completed job | Airtasker (about 22%) | Best aligned with both sides | Most leak-prone; needs in-app payment | Standardised, priced, one-off jobs |
| Provider subscription or AI tools | Justdial, Yelp's $30-a-month AI reply agent | Predictable, leak-proof | Hard to sell to micro providers before demand is proven | After demand density exists |
| Customer membership or maintenance plan | Thumbtack Plus, Mahir, Honey Homes | Creates frequency in an infrequent category | Narrows the top of the funnel in price-sensitive markets | Retention layer, not launch |
| Selling surplus demand to networks | eLocal's publisher ping API | Monetises requests you cannot fulfil | US only | Secondary line |

Sources: [Service Direct](https://blog.servicedirect.com/what-to-expect-when-buying-hvac-leads-from-service-direct), [Yelp via PPC Land](https://ppc.land/yelp-charges-30-a-month-for-ai-agent-that-answers-and-qualifies-leads-2/), [eLocal](https://elocal.com/docs/call_api/).

Taken together, the evidence supports a specific shape for a new entrant. Charge on an outcome the provider recognises as fair: a connected, qualified conversation or a booked appointment, with a published rule and automatic credits, and never for unanswered or declined contacts. Keep matches exclusive and consumer-chosen. Route contact through masked or in-app calling and chat, so the app has the attribution and deduplication data a call-tracking platform has; Google's own rule of charging a repeat caller at most once in 15 days is a ready model ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-lsa-missed-subsequent-calls-41940.html)). Put AI qualification in front of the hand-off. Launch in one city and one or two urgent, one-off trades (AC repair, plumbing, movers), where leakage is lowest and comparison is most valuable, and add recurring hooks later.

Two further openings are more speculative. One is the two-sided agent: US investors have put more than $125M into Avoca, valued at $1B, and about $43M into Netic for AI that answers the phone for contractors ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/avoca-125-million-raised-at-1-billion-valuation-to-power-services-economy-with-ai/); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-13/founders-fund-backs-startup-bringing-ai-to-plumbers-and-roofers)). A stripped-down WhatsApp or voice answering tool given to micro providers would address their missed calls and make them reachable by the app's consumer side, which also eases the cold start, because the app can serve demand by contacting providers who have never installed it. The other is becoming the verified, bookable supply behind AI assistants in markets Angi and Thumbtack do not cover.

The counter-case deserves weight. No funded independent consumer "quote agent" startup surfaced in the research, which may mean Google's free feature has closed that door in the US. Consumer habits in home services have so far changed only under heavy subsidy. And the central design tension has no clean answer: the more the platform sets prices, assigns workers and holds payment (which is what reduces leakage and quality variance), the more it resembles an employer and a payments business. Worker classification was Homejoy's stated cause of death. The rules on escrow and payment licensing, call recording, data privacy and automated messaging in India, Pakistan, the UAE and Saudi Arabia were not researched and need legal review before any payment-holding feature is built.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the idea. "Middleman between customers and providers" describes four different businesses, and three of them are crowded or closing: listing providers (Google does it free), selling introductions (Angi's numbers show what that revenue was really made of) and collecting quotes by AI (Google ships it at no charge in the US). The fourth, guaranteeing that an introduction turns into a completed, paid, reviewed job, is the one nobody at scale owns, and Google walked away from it when it dropped its guarantee. The same shift is visible inside pay-per-call, where the best-paying categories have already moved from billing for a call to billing for an appointment.

The choice of geography matters more than the choice of feature. In the US the price benchmarks are known ($50 to $120 per lead, roughly $116 per booked job) and the fight is over fairness and conversion against well-funded incumbents. In Pakistan, India and the Gulf the benchmarks are missing, lead values are a fraction of US levels and the incumbent is an informal referral network that charges nothing. The first thing to test there is not the app but the fee: whether a small provider will pay anything per booked job when a WhatsApp referral is free, and whether a customer will keep paying through the app on the second visit. A manual, WhatsApp-run pilot in one neighbourhood and one trade would answer both questions before any code is written.
