## 1. Player by player: model, pricing, revenue, profitability, scale

### Takeaway
Three model families dominate: (a) pay-per-lead / advertising directories (Angi, Thumbtack, Yelp, Justdial, Bark, Checkatrade, Google LSA) which are the largest by revenue and mostly profitable but generate the most provider complaints; (b) commission-on-completed-job marketplaces (Airtasker ~22% take, TaskRabbit ~15% service fee plus ~7.5% trust fee); (c) managed/full-stack services (Urban Company ~25-30% commission plus product sales, Frontdoor warranty). The biggest US lead-gen incumbent (Angi) is shrinking (~$1.03B revenue in 2025, down 13%) while Thumbtack (~$500M, +24%) and Urban Company (+36%) grow; Urban Company's core is profitable but its new instant-maid vertical pushed it back into losses.

### Inferences
- Revenue scale ranking of what is verifiable: Frontdoor (~$2.2B guidance, a warranty/insurance product) > Angi (~$1.0B, shrinking) > Yelp services ads (~$0.95B annualised) > Thumbtack (~$0.5B, growing) > Urban Company (~$185M at ~Rs 84/USD) > Justdial (~$145M) > Airtasker (~US$38M). The biggest pools of money are in advertising/lead-gen and prepaid warranty, not in commission-on-job marketplaces.
- Commission marketplaces that actually process payment cluster around a 20-25% headline take (Airtasker ~22%, TaskRabbit ~22.5% combined client-side fees, Urban Company up to 25% headline commission but ~36% all-in revenue/NTV once product sales and fees are counted).
- Emerging-market players outside India are tiny by funding (Justlife ~$2M, Sheba ~$3M), suggesting venture capital has not found a scalable model there, or that these businesses run on cash flow.
- Porch and Frontdoor show two exits from the pure marketplace: pivot to insurance (Porch) or bundle services into a prepaid plan (Frontdoor).

## 2. Lead-gen vs managed marketplace vs full-stack: comparative unit economics

### Takeaway
Lead-gen has 90%+ gross margins and no payment/quality liability but misaligns incentives (the platform is paid whether or not the pro wins), which drives pro churn and regulatory risk; commission and managed models align incentives and produce high repeat rates (Urban Company ~82% of NTV from repeat customers) but need 20-36% all-in take, heavy training/supply spend, and they face leakage and labour unrest. Direct CAC and churn disclosures are scarce; the best quantified evidence is Angi's pro base shrinking 19% in one year.

### Cited Findings
- Thumbtack reports 90%+ gross margins and EBITDA profitability since 2023 on a pay-per-lead model — [Built In](https://builtin.com/company/thumbtack/faq/stability-growth)
- In pay-per-lead, the pro pays per lead accessed whether or not the job closes, and competes with multiple other pros on the same lead — [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/?p=83716)
- Worked example of lead-gen economics from the pro's side: a $60 Angi lead at a 20% close rate implies $300 acquisition cost per closed job; whether shared-lead or take-rate pricing is cheaper for a pro depends on close rate and ticket size (vendor source with its own interest) — [AskBaily](https://askbaily.pages.dev/tools/lead-economics)
- UK equivalents: real cost per customer won via Checkatrade-type directories is estimated at GBP 80-200 (competitor estimate) — [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/trade-lead-break-even-uk/)
- Angi's own framing converts lead fees into a take rate (fees / value of jobs won and completed), acknowledging that pros evaluate lead-gen as an implicit commission — [Angi CEO note, snippet only](https://intercom.help/angi/en/articles/10274582-a-note-from-angi-ceo-jeff-kip)
- Angi provider churn signal: average monthly active pros -19% to ~122,000 and new pro acquisition -36% in 2025 (secondary) — [Panabee](https://www.panabee.com/news/angi-earnings-2025-annual)
- Commission marketplace take rate: Airtasker monetisation rate ~21.7-22.2% of GMV in FY26 — [ASX announcement](https://bulletin.webull.com/qbd/announcement/20260826/499500381/09acc31376e150e92d7a425d9f6e2ba9.pdf)
- Managed/full-stack: Urban Company FY26 revenue Rs 1,555.5 crore on NTV Rs 4,290 crore (~36% all-in); headline commission up to 25% with product sales and fees taking the total to nearly 40% — [Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/corporate-news/English/tech/Urban-Company-FY26-Revenue-Surges-36percent-to-indian-rupee1556-Cr-Loss-Narrows/69fdbb0b81f0ecc2dc72f3e7); [Al Jazeera 2024 (mirror)](https://aljazeeranews-3fuh52rgrl.edgeone.app/economy/2024/3/1/how-indias-urban-company-has-soured-gig-work-for-women)
- Managed-model repeat rate: ~82% of Urban Company NTV from repeat customers — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/start-ups/urban-company-fy25-profit-revenue-growth-ipo-plans-125061801031_1.html)
- Managed-model profitability took about a decade: Urban Company (founded 2014) posted its first full-year profit in FY25, and core (ex-InstaHelp) adjusted EBITDA reached Rs 106 crore in FY26 — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/06/urban-company-first-full-year-profit-revenue-jumps-38-ipo); [Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/corporate-news/English/tech/Urban-Companys-Q4-NTV-Surges-42percent-FY26-Profit-Jumps-Ninefold/69fdbaac81f0ecc2dc72f1fc)
- High-frequency categories are expensive to seed even for the incumbent: InstaHelp lost Rs 61 crore on 1.61M orders in one quarter (about Rs 380 loss per order, calculated) — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/urban-company-q3-fy26-revenue-jumps-to-383-cr-losses-persist-amid-instahelp-expansion)
- Opinion: disintermediation risk rises with take rate, becoming a much bigger problem at 40%+ (commenter opinion, not data) — [Valuepunks Substack comments](https://valuepunks.substack.com/p/small-cap-idea-rover-rovr/comments)
- Dan Hockenmaier (ex-Thumbtack growth, Faire) argues retention often matters more than acquisition in marketplace growth models — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/developing-a-growth-model-marketplace)

### Inferences
- Model fit follows job type. Lead-gen suits high-ticket, unstandardised, quote-required jobs (roofing, remodels) where a $30-90 lead is a small share of a multi-thousand-dollar job and where on-platform payment is unrealistic. Commission/managed suits standardised, priceable, repeatable jobs (cleaning, beauty, appliance service, assembly) where instant booking with upfront price is possible.
- A 20-25% take is sustainable only if the platform supplies things the pro cannot get alone: steady demand, payment, insurance/guarantee, training, tools or consumables. Urban Company's extra ~10 points come from selling products and consumables to partners, which doubles as quality control.
- Lead-gen's weak point is that revenue can grow while pro ROI falls; the correction (Angi 2025-26) is painful and public. A founder choosing lead-gen should cap pros per lead and price on a win-rate basis from day one.

### Gaps
- No reliable published CAC, LTV or cohort retention figures for any player were found. Urban Company's DRHP and shareholder letters likely contain marketing spend as % of NTV and cohort data, but I did not open them.
- No audited provider churn rates. Unverified recollection: the FTC complaint against HomeAdvisor referenced very high annual service-provider turnover; this needs checking in the FTC documents.
- Hockenmaier's and a16z/NFX's specific writing on the evolution of service marketplaces (lead-gen to managed) was not retrieved.

## 3. Failure modes and notable shutdowns

### Takeaway
The recurring killers are leakage after the first job, lead-quality misalignment, labour classification and partner unrest, and low frequency. Homejoy (2015) is the canonical case: officially killed by worker-classification lawsuits, but former staff also blamed costly international expansion and its best cleaners leaving for direct arrangements with customers. In India, almost every 2014-15 home-services entrant except Urban Company was shut, acquired or shrunk.

### Cited Findings
- Homejoy (founded 2010, ~$40M raised) shut in July 2015; co-founder Adora Cheung called four worker-misclassification lawsuits the "deciding factor", and the litigation coincided with a failed fundraise — [Time](https://time.com/3963565/homejoy-cleaning-shuts-down/); [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/248579); [TechCrunch 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/12/16/the-missing-leak-in-marketplaces/)
- A former Homejoy employee cited other causes: a costly international expansion and leakage of its best workers into direct employment arrangements with customers — [Wikipedia: Homejoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homejoy)
- TechCrunch's "The Missing Leak in Marketplaces" argues that TaskRabbit and Homejoy looked like marketplaces but behaved like lead-gen, because once customer and provider meet, repeat business goes off-platform — [TechCrunch 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/12/16/the-missing-leak-in-marketplaces/)
- Pakistan example of the same mechanism: Dawn on Sukoon.com.pk notes that after a few dealings the customer and worker build enough rapport to cut the platform out and save the commission; Sukoon later closed — [Dawn 2017](https://www.dawn.com/news/1309231/can-pakistani-home-repair-startup-sukooncompk-live-up-to-its-name); [Dawn, "Lights out at Sukoon"](https://www.dawn.com/news/1530589/lights-out-at-sukoon)
- Leakage is called the paramount threat to service marketplaces because the inventory is the provider's time; penalties alone do not work, the platform's value must be a fair trade for its fee — [Platform Chronicles](https://platformchronicles.substack.com/p/platform-leakage-ii-the-good-the)
- Sharetribe's guidance: users go around the payment system when the platform adds no value at transaction time; the cure is transaction-level value (payment protection, insurance, scheduling, reviews) rather than policing; a practitioner guide adds that there is no hermetic fix, only mitigation — [Sharetribe Academy](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/how-to-discourage-people-from-going-around-your-payment-system/); [Mobile Spoon](https://mobilespoon.net/2019/08/minimizing-leakage-marketplace-offline.html)
- Peer-reviewed treatment of the problem exists ("Marketplace Leakage", Management Science 2024) — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.4757)
- India's 2014-15 cohort: UrbanClap, Housejoy, Zimmber, LocalOye, Taskbob, Doormint, EasyFix and Timesaverz launched within about 18 months; Doormint shut down, EasyFix was acquired, Zimmber was bought by Quikr, LocalOye went through layoffs and restructuring, Housejoy survived only by repeatedly reshaping its business — [A Junior VC, Mar 2026](https://www.ajuniorvc.com/insant-helps-snabbit-pronto-case-study-urban-company-market-unicorn)
- Labour/partner unrest at the managed-model winner: Urban Company cut its top beauty commission slab from 30% to 25% and capped monthly penalties at Rs 1,500 after partner protests in Oct 2021; it sued four protesters in 2021; 200+ partners protested permanent ID blocking and security-deposit demands; a 2024 Bengaluru protest ended after a legal notice — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/urban-company-slashes-commission-by-5-percentage-after-workers-protest/65384.html); [Business & Human Rights Resource Centre](https://www.business-humanrights.org/ko/최신-뉴스/india-urban-company-reduces-commission-cap-in-12-point-agenda-following-protests/); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/urban-company-rocked-by-another-protest-by-gig-workers-for-blocking-their-ids); [YourStory 2024](https://yourstory.com/2024/06/urban-company-acknowledges-grievances-sent-by-protesting-partners-in-bengaluru-plans-legal-action)
- Lead-quality failure with legal consequences: FTC v. HomeAdvisor (see section 4) — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/23/the-ftc-fines-homeadvisor-up-to-7-2m-for-lying-about-lead-quality-and-other-matters)
- Macro failure in Pakistan's startup scene generally: Airlift ceased operations in 2022 citing global recession and capital-market downturn; Careem suspended ride-hailing in Pakistan in 2025 (context, not home services) — [Arab News](https://arabnews.pk/node/2121501/pakistan); [Arab News on Careem](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2604892/pakistan)

### Inferences
- Leakage is worst where the same provider returns to the same home (cleaning, maids, tutoring, beauty) and mildest for one-off or emergency jobs with changing providers. Models that survive in recurring categories either rotate/assign providers and own the customer relationship (Urban Company, InstaHelp) or stop charging per job and charge the pro a fixed fee/subscription (directories).
- Classification risk scales with control: the more a platform sets prices, scripts and penalties (which quality requires), the more its "partners" look like employees and the more organised protest it attracts.
- In low-trust, cash-heavy, WhatsApp-native markets such as Pakistan, a per-job commission is the easiest fee to evade; the Sukoon evidence points that way.

### Gaps
- Amazon Home Services: no source found for shutdown date or stated reasons.
- Handy's decline inside Angi: not sourced this session.
- Root causes for each Indian shutdown (Doormint, Taskbob, Zimmber) were not found beyond their outcomes; Housejoy is reported as surviving in reshaped form, not shut down, so the "Housejoy shutdown" premise is unconfirmed.
- Reasons for Sukoon's closure are in a Dawn article I could not open.
- No quantified leakage rates (share of repeat jobs going off-platform) found for any platform.

## 4. Angi "homeowner choice", Thumbtack instant match and AI, FTC v. HomeAdvisor

### Takeaway
Regulators and economics both pushed US lead-gen away from blasting one request to many pros. Angi's January 2025 move to homeowner choice (the customer selects which pre-screened pros get the request) cut Network-channel revenue by 56-79% and total revenue by 13% in 2025, with declines continuing through mid-2026. Thumbtack, which moved to automatic instant-match lead charging in 2017 and drew pro anger over price, is growing 24% and has become an early distribution partner inside ChatGPT.

### Cited Findings
- Homeowner choice was implemented in January 2025 and lets customers select among pre-screened professionals rather than being auto-matched to several — [Panabee Q4 2025](https://www.panabee.com/news/angi-earnings-q4-2025); [Angi IR Q4 2025](https://ir.angi.com/node/13006/html)
- Impact: Q4 2025 Network service requests -69% and Network leads -78% YoY; Network revenue -79%; meanwhile Proprietary service requests +15%, Proprietary leads +25% and Proprietary revenue +23% — [Angi IR Q4 2025](https://ir.angi.com/node/13006/html)
- FY2025 revenue -13% to ~$1.03B; Q1 2026 Network revenue still -56%; Q2 2026 revenue -11%; formal guidance withdrawn — [Panabee annual](https://www.panabee.com/news/angi-earnings-2025-annual); [Quartr Q1](https://quartr.com/events/angi-inc-angi-q1-2026_3e5aFGrf); [Quartr Q2](https://quartr.com/events/angi-inc-angi-q2-2026_3e5aFEBu)
- Management frames the change as a short-term revenue hit that improves customer satisfaction and pro win rates, with re-acceleration expected in 2027 on AI products — [StockAnalysis transcripts](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/angi/transcripts/); [Quartr Q2](https://quartr.com/events/angi-inc-angi-q2-2026_3e5aFEBu)
- Thumbtack Instant Match (2017) automatically charges pros for matching leads; pros report that lead costs rose sharply at launch — [Handyman Startup](https://handymanstartup.com/thumbtack-pro-reviews); [InvoiceOwl](https://www.invoiceowl.com/?p=28454)
- Thumbtack + OpenAI (announced 6 Oct 2025): US ChatGPT users asking about home maintenance, repair or improvement can be shown Thumbtack pros via its APIs; Thumbtack was among the first app integrations in ChatGPT — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251006760229/en/Thumbtack-Partners-with-OpenAI-to-Power-Home-Services-in-ChatGPT); [Wikipedia mirror](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Thumbtack_(company))
- Thumbtack is also building AI that diagnoses a home problem from the customer's description before matching — [Pulse summary of coverage](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/news/thumbtacks-new-ai-wants-to-diagnose-your-leaky-ceiling-a82fa256-ebad-4659-b533-a260dc40b19e/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91534514/)
- FTC v. HomeAdvisor: the FTC alleged that since at least mid-2014 HomeAdvisor made false, misleading or unsubstantiated claims about the source and quality of its leads and that providers wasted time on bad leads and then on chasing refunds — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/80884); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/homeadvisor-penalized-by-ftc-for-alleged-false-claims-about-leads/486906/)
- Outcome: order for up to $7.2M in redress (a ceiling, technically not a fine), finalised 21 April 2023; up to $30 per provider misled on lead quality and up to $59.99 for those told the first month of mHelpDesk was free; HomeAdvisor is barred from misrepresenting leads and admitted no wrongdoing; claim deadline was 26 Feb 2024 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/23/the-ftc-fines-homeadvisor-up-to-7-2m-for-lying-about-lead-quality-and-other-matters); [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/topics/regulation-and-compliance/ftc-finalizes-order-against-homeadvisor-for-deceptive-marketing); [Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/consumer-products/subscriptions/homeadvisor-false-advertising-ftc-refunds/)

### Inferences
- Angi's numbers put a price on shared leads: a large part of the old model's revenue came from selling the same request to pros the homeowner never chose. Removing that cost roughly an eighth of total revenue in one year and almost all of the affiliate/Network channel.
- The lesson for a new app is to start where Angi was forced to end: customer-selected or single-pro matching, with pricing tied to verified contact or booked job.
- AI assistants are becoming a new demand front door. A marketplace with an API and structured supply (availability, price, reviews) can be surfaced inside ChatGPT; one without it risks being bypassed.

### Gaps
- Whether the FCC's TCPA "one-to-one consent" rule drove Angi's homeowner-choice decision could not be sourced this session. Unverified recollection: Angi cited that rule, which was later vacated by a court in early 2025, and kept the change anyway.
- The Angi CEO note with take-rate specifics could not be opened (intercom.help is outside this session's network allowlist).
- Other lawsuits (contractor class actions against HomeAdvisor, state attorney-general actions, Thumbtack litigation) were not retrieved.
- Angi Q3 2026 results (due early Nov 2026) were not found.

## 5. What providers and customers complain about most

### Takeaway
Provider complaints are remarkably uniform across countries: paying for leads that never answer or never hire, the same lead sold to several competitors, rising and opaque lead prices, automatic charges, out-of-area or mismatched leads, and hard-to-get refunds. On managed platforms the complaints shift to commission level, penalties, arbitrary ID blocking and forced product purchases.

### Cited Findings
- Thumbtack pros pay per lead whether or not the job closes, face several competing pros, and describe leads as vague, low-budget or not ready to hire — [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/?p=83716)
- Lead prices jumped with Instant Match; lead fees can approach the job value on small jobs — [Handyman Startup](https://handymanstartup.com/thumbtack-pro-reviews); [SplitMetrics app reviews](https://splitmetrics.com/apps/thumbtack-for-professionals/id908795016)
- Pros report being charged for requests 100+ miles outside their service area — [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/homeowners/thumbtack.html?page=7)
- Customers do not realise the pro paid to talk to them, so they ghost freely; pros ask Thumbtack to tell customers about lead costs — [Thumbtack Community](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/comment/5845); [Thumbtack Community](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/comment/6496/)
- Thumbtack's stated remedy is credits for invalid contact details, service mismatch or out-of-area leads — [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/?p=96237)
- HomeAdvisor providers spent time on leads that did not pan out and then more time seeking refunds (FTC complaint) — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/80884)
- UK: Checkatrade renewal quotes can jump sharply; advertised "from" prices are rarely what trades pay; Bark shortened credit expiry to 3 months without refunds (competitor-authored sources) — [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/trades/checkatrade-cost-uk/); [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/trades-lead-generation-cost-uk/)
- Google LSA: message leads repriced upward in July 2025; loss of manual per-lead bid control in the 2026 migration — [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/google-lsas-equalize-message-lead-costs-in-july-2025-raising-small-business-expenses); [TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/google-local-services-ads-performance-max-migration-local-inventory-ads-default-2026)
- Urban Company partners: commission levels, penalties, permanent ID blocking, deposits to reopen blocked IDs, and pressure tied to ratings and acceptance metrics; reporting describes women partners saying they were pushed toward poverty — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/exclusive-troubles-erupt-for-urban-company-as-beauticians-protest-id-bans/); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/magazine/1014700/were-being-pushed-into-poverty-voices-of-women-who-took-on-the-unicorn-start-up-urban-company); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/in-depth/the-urban-cost-of-gig-service)
- TaskRabbit clients pay stacked fees (service fee plus trust and support fee) on top of the Tasker's rate — [TaskRabbit support](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/articles/204940570)

### Inferences
- The single most damaging design choice is charging the provider before any sign of customer intent, combined with customers who face no cost for ghosting. Anything that adds customer-side commitment (selecting the pro, a small booking deposit, a confirmed time slot) attacks the top complaint directly.
- Transparency about what the pro paid, and automatic rather than claim-based refunds for dead leads, would address the next two.

### Gaps
- No Reddit, BBB or contractor-forum threads were retrieved directly; the evidence is from review sites, Thumbtack's own forum and vendor blogs, several of which are competitors with an incentive to exaggerate.
- Consumer-side complaints (no-shows, poor workmanship, price surprises, unsolicited calls after submitting a request) were not specifically sourced this session.
- No complaint data for Justdial, Sulekha, Bark (beyond the credit change) or Middle East/Pakistan platforms.

## 6. Trends 2024-2026

### Takeaway
The category is being reshaped by AI front doors (ChatGPT, Yelp Assistant, Thumbtack's diagnostic AI, Angi's AI roadmap), by Google absorbing local services leads into automated Performance Max bidding, and in India by a race to instant, upfront-priced, high-frequency house help (InstaHelp vs Snabbit vs Pronto) that is growing fast and losing money.

### Cited Findings
- ChatGPT now recommends Thumbtack pros in-conversation (Oct 2025) — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251006760229/en/Thumbtack-Partners-with-OpenAI-to-Power-Home-Services-in-ChatGPT)
- Yelp Assistant generates ~10-15% of Request-a-Quote projects in 2026, and Yelp's fastest-growing line (+82%) includes Hatch, an AI lead-response product sold to service businesses — [Motley Fool Q1 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/08/yelp-yelp-q1-2026-earnings-transcript/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/yelp-ad-revenue-drops-6-as-clicks-fall-8-in-third-quarter/)
- Angi ties its 2027 growth outlook to AI products — [Quartr Q2 2026](https://quartr.com/events/angi-inc-angi-q2-2026_3e5aFEBu)
- Google is folding Local Services Ads into Google Ads/Performance Max from Aug 2026, keeping pay-per-lead but removing manual max-per-lead bids — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-folds-local-services-ads-into-google-ads-cuts-historical-reports/); [Invoca](https://www.invoca.com/blog/googles-local-services-ads-move-to-performance-max-what-home-services-businesses-need-to-know)
- India's instant house-help race: Urban Company's InstaHelp reached 1.61M orders in one quarter with a Rs 61 crore loss, competing with Snabbit and Pronto — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/urban-company-q3-fy26-revenue-jumps-to-383-cr-losses-persist-amid-instahelp-expansion); [A Junior VC](https://www.ajuniorvc.com/insant-helps-snabbit-pronto-case-study-urban-company-market-unicorn)
- Urban Company's annual transacting users rose to nearly 10M (+35% YoY) by Q2 FY27, and Q1 FY27 revenue grew 44%, with investors rewarding growth despite losses — [UC Q2 FY27 letter](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/URBANCO_16102026160706_Urban_Company_Shareholders_Letter_Q2_FY27.pdf); [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/stocks/urban-company-q1-fy-2026-27-results-revenue-up-shares-jump-despite-loss/)
- Field-service software vendors (Housecall Pro, Jobber) publish guides on using and evaluating Thumbtack/Angi leads, positioning themselves as the pro's system of record around third-party lead sources — [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/what-is-thumbtack-how-it-works/); [Jobber Academy](https://getjobber.com/academy/thumbtack-vs-taskrabbit/)
- Distribution via super-apps in the Gulf: Justlife services are offered inside Careem — [tbreak](https://tbreak.com/?p=5816)
- Marketplaces are adding non-marketplace revenue: Yelp (Hatch, data licensing), Porch (insurance), Urban Company (products), Frontdoor (non-warranty services) — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/yelp-ad-revenue-drops-6-as-clicks-fall-8-in-third-quarter/); [Porch Q2 2026](https://s29.q4cdn.com/135224588/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/PRCH-Q2-2026-Earnings-Press-Release.pdf); [Yahoo/StockStory on Frontdoor](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/ftdr-q1-deep-dive-member-020455641.html)

### Inferences
- Demand is fragmenting upstream of the marketplace (AI assistants, Google, super-apps). The defensible assets are structured, bookable supply (real availability, fixed prices, verified quality) and the transaction layer, not the directory.
- Frequency is the strategic prize: Urban Company is willing to lose roughly Rs 380 per order to own a daily-use category, because frequency fixes CAC and repeat economics for everything else it sells.
- For pros, the tool they open every day (scheduling/invoicing software, or WhatsApp in emerging markets) is the natural place for leads to arrive; a new app that requires a separate inbox is at a disadvantage.

- No sourced evidence on WhatsApp-based service marketplaces or booking flows in Pakistan, the Gulf or Southeast Asia.
- Details and metrics of Snabbit and Pronto (funding, order volumes, pricing) were not retrieved beyond their mention as InstaHelp competitors.
- Angi's and Thumbtack's specific AI agent/concierge products and their conversion impact were not retrieved in detail.
